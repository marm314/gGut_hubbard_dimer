\section{The $\Ng=0$ case (ordinary Gutzwiller)}
\label{sec:ng0}

At $\dV=0$ the Gutzwiller equations of the dimer have a closed solution (\texttt{gGut\_notes.pdf}, Sec.~III\,D). With $V=-tR$ and $\lambda=\lambda^c=0$ the embedding ground state gives, for $U\le U_c=8t$ (Brinkman--Rice),
\begin{equation}
  Z=R^2=1-\Bigl(\frac{U}{8t}\Bigr)^2,\qquad
  E=-2t-\frac U2-\frac{U^2}{32t},\qquad
  \langle n_\uparrow n_\downarrow\rangle=\frac14-\frac{U}{32t},\qquad
  P_{\rm ion}=\frac12-\frac{U}{16t},
\end{equation}
and for $U\ge8t$ only $R=0$ exists: $Z=0$, $E=-U$, $P_{\rm ion}=0$. The quasiparticle Hamiltonian is $-tZ\,\sigma_x$, with levels $\pm tZ$ and orbitals $(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$ on the two states; each level carries the weight $2Z$, so the spectral function consists of two peaks, at $\pm tZ$ with weight $2Z$,
that move to $\omega=0$ and vanish at $U_c$ (the gap $2tZ$ closes continuously). Here $U_c$ is the value where the spectral function disappears. The closed forms agree with the numerical $\Ng=0$ solution of the code to $1.4\times10^{-7}$ in $Z$ and $10^{-8}$ in $E$ and $\langle n_\uparrow n_\downarrow\rangle$ (script \texttt{ng0\_analysis.py}).

At $\dV\neq0$ particle--hole symmetry is broken, so $\lambda\neq0$ and the embedding problem is $3\times3$ in the states $S=(a^\dagger_\uparrow d^\dagger_\downarrow-a^\dagger_\downarrow d^\dagger_\uparrow)/\sqrt2$, $D=a^\dagger_\uparrow a^\dagger_\downarrow$, $E=d^\dagger_\uparrow d^\dagger_\downarrow$ of the physical ($a$) and bath ($d$) orbitals.
The site exchange combined with the particle--hole transformation relates the two fragments, $\phi_1=(s,\epsilon,\delta)$ for $\phi_0=(s,\delta,\epsilon)$ in the basis $(S,D,E)$, so $Z_0=Z_1$ and the problem has two variables, $c=\delta^2+\epsilon^2$ and $m=\delta^2-\epsilon^2$. With $r=\sqrt{c^2-m^2}$:
\begin{equation}
  E=-U(1-c)-2\dV\,m-4t\,\frac{(1-c)(c+r)}{\sqrt{1-m^2}},\qquad
  Z=\frac{2(1-c)(c+r)}{1-m^2},\qquad P_{\rm ion}=c,\qquad n=1+m,
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the number of electrons on the favored site; the quasiparticle levels are $\pm tZ/\sqrt{1-m^2}$ with weight $2Z$ each ($\lambda=tZm/\sqrt{1-m^2}$), and at $\dV=0$ ($m=0$) all of this reduces to the formulas above.
The stationarity conditions, $U\sqrt{1-m^2}\,r=4t(c+r)(1-c-r)$ and $\dV(1-m^2)^{3/2}r=2t(1-c)\,m\,(1-c^2-rc)$, are solved for $(c,m)$; they reproduce the numerical $\Ng=0$ solution of the code (\texttt{scan\_ng0/}) to $8\times10^{-8}$ in $E$, $4\times10^{-7}$ in $Z$ and $5\times10^{-8}$ in $P_{\rm ion}$ (script \texttt{ng0\_dV\_analytic.py}).
$Z$ vanishes for $c,m\to0$ with $q=r/c$ fixed, where the two conditions give $U_c=4t(1+q)/q$ and $\dV=2t\sqrt{1-q^2}/q$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  U_c=4t+2\sqrt{4t^2+\dV^2},
\end{equation}
which is $8t$ at $\dV=0$, $9.66\,t$ at $\dV=2$ and grows as $2\dV$ for large $\dV$.

\paragraph{Quasiparticle orbitals.} In the basis of the two states $H_{qp}=-\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda & tZ\\ tZ & -\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $\lambda=tZ\,m/\sqrt{1-m^2}$, so the levels are $\mp E_q$, $E_q=tZ/\sqrt{1-m^2}$, and the orbitals (up to a global sign, chosen as that of the $\Ng=2$ HOMO and LUMO on the two states) are
\begin{equation}
  |{\rm occ}\rangle=\Bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac{1+m}{2}},\ \sqrt{\tfrac{1-m}{2}}\Bigr),\qquad
  |{\rm virt}\rangle=\Bigl(\sqrt{\tfrac{1-m}{2}},\ -\sqrt{\tfrac{1+m}{2}}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the occupied orbital has the density $(1+m)/2$ on the favored state ($0.945$, $0.759$ and $0.549$ at $U/t=0.1$, $4$ and $8$ for $\dV=2$). At $\dV=0$, $m=0$ and the orbitals are $(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$.
Two limits are explicit: for $U\to0$, $m\to\dV/\sqrt{t^2+\dV^2}$, the non-interacting value ($0.894$ for $\dV=2$; the solution gives $0.894$ at $U/t=0.01$); at $U_c$, $m\to0$ and $Z\to0$, so the orbitals become $(1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$ with levels at zero.
For general $(U,\dV)$, $m$ is not an explicit function of $(U,\dV)$; instead $U$ and $\dV$ are explicit functions of $(c,m)$,
\begin{equation}
  U=\frac{4t\,(c+r)(1-c-r)}{\sqrt{1-m^2}\,r},\qquad
  \dV=\frac{2t\,(1-c)\,m\,(1-c^2-rc)}{(1-m^2)^{3/2}\,r},\qquad r=\sqrt{c^2-m^2},
\end{equation}
which is the stationarity condition solved for $(U,\dV)$; for given $(U,\dV)$ the pair $(c,m)$ is its root (script \texttt{ng0\_analytic.py}; the round trip $(U,\dV)\to(c,m)\to(U,\dV)$ closes to $10^{-6}$).

\noindent In summary, $m=\delta^2-\epsilon^2$ (half the difference of the electron numbers of the two states) and the amplitudes $\delta^2=(c+m)/2$, $\epsilon^2=(c-m)/2$ are known in closed form only at $\dV=0$ ($m=0$, $\delta=\epsilon=\sqrt{1/4-U/32t}$ for $U\le8t$), in the limit $U\to0$ (above), and as $U\to U_c$ where $\delta,\epsilon\to0$ with $m/c\to\dV/\sqrt{4t^2+\dV^2}$; for all other $(U,\dV)$ they are given parametrically by the two equations above and obtained numerically from them. The critical $U_c/t$ is in closed form for all $\dV$.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
The number of quasiparticle branches is $2(1+\Ng)$: two for $\Ng=0$ and six for $\Ng=2$. In all four panels the gGA peaks coincide with the
ED ones at small $U/t$ and move away from them as $U/t$ grows, consistent with
the error in the total energy growing with $U/t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}). For $\Ng=0$ there is a single positive peak, at $tZ$ with weight $2Z$ at $\dV=0$, which goes to $\omega=0$ and vanishes at $U_c/t=8.00$ ($9.66$ at $\dV=2$): the spectral function disappears.
For $\Ng=2$ the extra branches give the outer peaks, and the inner peak vanishes at the lower $U_c/t=6.56$ ($8.33$ at $\dV=2$). The effect of $\dV$ is
visible for both $\Ng$: at $\dV=2$ the branches are well separated
at small $U/t$ and bunch together as $U/t$ increases, with a rearrangement for $\Ng=2$
between $U/t=0.6$ and $0.7$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Quasiparticle orbitals and density: the gap closing at $U_c$}

Two more movies per case show the eigenorbitals of $H_{qp}$ and the quasiparticle density as $U/t$ grows ($\Ng=0$ and $2$; $\dV=0,2$; script \texttt{make\_qp\_orbital\_videos.py}).
They are drawn on the skeleton of the dimer: the two large circles joined left to right are the two states (the direction of $R_s$, the only qp orbital that hops to the other site), and the small outer
circles are their ghosts (the on-site eigenvectors orthogonal to it). Occupation as in the code (\texttt{gga\_dimer.qp\_state}): the lowest $N/2$ eigenvectors returned by \texttt{eigh} are filled,
without fractional occupation. In the orbital movies each panel follows one orbital as $U/t$ grows (it is matched to the previous $U/t$ by maximum overlap, so it does not jump when levels cross or split; the orbitals of degenerate levels are rotated onto the previous ones); the title gives its occupation, energy and $A(\omega)$ weight. Circle area is $|\text{amplitude}|^2$ and
the color is the sign (the signs of the orbitals and of the ghost circles are aligned with the previous $U/t$, so a color change is a change of the orbital, not of the phase). In the density movies the colors give the qp density per orbital (the code's $\rho$), next to the gaps, bond order, $Z$ and double occupancy (or state density
at $\dV=2$) as functions of $U/t$; the dashed line is $U_c/t$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $\Ng=0$, $\dV=0$: \href{run:../scan_ng0/dV_0.0/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng0/dV_0.0/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=0$, $\dV=2$: \href{run:../scan_ng0/dV_2.0/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng0/dV_2.0/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=2$, $\dV=0$: \href{run:../scan_ng2_not_imp/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng2_not_imp/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=2$, $\dV=2$: \href{run:../scan_ng2_dV2_confirmed/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng2_dV2_confirmed/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
\end{itemize}
What can be seen at the critical $U_c/t$ of the weight transfer ($6.56$ for $\dV=0$, $\simeq8.3$ for $\dV=2$):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Spectral gap} (first panel of the density movies, lowest positive minus highest negative energy among the levels with weight $\ge10^{-3}$): it decreases smoothly with $U/t$
  ($2.0$ at $U/t=0.1$, $1.14$ at $4$, $0.27$ at $6$ for $\dV=0$; $4.39$, $2.0$ (at $U/t=4$) and $1.19$ (at $6$) for $\dV=2$, $\Ng=2$) and closes at $U_c$; the next grid point has a large gap
  ($0.03\to4.94$ between $U/t=6.5$ and $6.6$ at $\dV=0$; $0.001\to4.36$ between $8.5$ and $8.6$ at $\dV=2$, $\Ng=2$), and it grows afterwards (at $U/t=10$: $8.59$ and $5.76$).
  The qp gap (HOMO--LUMO) is zero from $U_c$ on; before $U_c$ it follows the spectral gap, except at small $U/t$, where weightless ghost levels lie inside the gap.
  \item \emph{Orbitals}: below $U_c$ the HOMO and LUMO have amplitude on the two states and carry $A(\omega)$ weight; from $U_c$ on, the orbitals at $\epsilon=0$ have no amplitude on the states and zero weight, and sit on ghost circles only
  (two of them). Which of these degenerate orbitals are occupied is the choice of \texttt{eigh}, so the occupied ghost orbitals change from frame to frame above $U_c$.
  \item \emph{Bond order and double occupancy} (center--center density matrix element, per spin): both change slope at $U_c$ (at $\dV=0$: bond order $0.17$, $0.09$, $0.08$ and double occupancy $0.040$, $0.016$, $0.013$ at $U/t=6$, $6.5$, $6.6$).
  At $\dV=2$ the bond order has a maximum ($0.40$) at $U/t\simeq4.3$ and falls to $0.08$ at $U_c$.
  \item \emph{State density at $\dV=2$}: the densities of the two states are polarized at small $U/t$ ($0.945$ and $0.055$ at $U/t=0.1$, $0.63$ and $0.37$ at $6$) and become equal at $U_c$ ($0.50$ and $0.50$, $\Ng=2$), staying equal above.
  \item $Z$ stays $\ge0.975$ for all $U/t$ (no collapse of the renormalization), and at $\dV=0$ the state densities are $0.50$ at all $U/t$; the colors of the ghost circles are not a signature of the transition.
\end{itemize}
This is a gap closing and re-opening of the gGA solution. It is not present in the exact dimer, whose spectral gap varies smoothly with $U/t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}).

\paragraph{Link with the spectral weights.} The hole peaks of $A(\omega)$ are photoemission lines: an electron is removed from the dimer and the cation is left in its bonding (lowest) or
antibonding one-electron orbital; the inner pole is the bonding final state and the outer pole the antibonding one. The intensity of each line is the overlap between that final state and the state obtained by removing an
electron from the ground state. At $\dV=0$ write the ground state as $\cos\theta\,|{\rm cov}\rangle+\sin\theta\,|{\rm ion}\rangle$ ($P_{\rm ion}=\sin^2\theta$). Removing an electron from the covalent part leaves the other electron on the
\emph{other} site, with amplitude $\propto\cos\theta$; removing it from the ionic part leaves the remaining electron on the \emph{same} site, with amplitude $\propto\sin\theta$. The two contributions interfere:
they add in the bonding orbital and subtract in the antibonding one, so that
\[
w_{\rm inner}=(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)^2=1+2\sqrt{P_{\rm ion}(1-P_{\rm ion})},\qquad
w_{\rm outer}=(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)^2=1-2\sqrt{P_{\rm ion}(1-P_{\rm ion})},
\]
which are the exact weights $1\pm4t/\Omega$, $\Omega=\sqrt{U^2+16t^2}$ (checked numerically). For example, at $U/t=4$, $\cos\theta=0.924$ and $\sin\theta=0.383$ ($P_{\rm ion}=0.146$) give $1.707$ and $0.293$.
The imbalance between the two lines is thus the product of the covalent and ionic amplitudes: it is maximal at $U=0$ ($P_{\rm ion}=\tfrac12$, weights $2$ and $0$, the one-electron picture) and it disappears when the state is purely covalent
(both weights equal to $1$, the two Hubbard-band-like lines). Transferring weight from the inner to the outer pole as $U/t$ grows is the same process as transferring weight from ionic to covalent. At $\dV=2$ the same interference holds but the ionic amplitudes of the two sites differ, so the weights depend on three amplitudes
(covalent, ionic on each site).

\section{Discussion: \texorpdfstring{$\dV=0$}{dV=0} vs. \texorpdfstring{$\dV=2$}{dV=2}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Weak coupling: $\dV$ dominates.}
At small $U/t$ the two scans differ substantially. At $U/t=0.1$,
$E_{\rm var}/t = -2.0503$ for $\dV=0$ but $-4.4823$ for $\dV=2$.
This is the expected $U\to0$ limit: with $\dV=0$ the dimer's noninteracting
energy is $-2t=-2$ plus a small $O(U)$ correction, while at $\dV=2$ the
site-splitting opens a one-body level gap and the $U\to0$ energy approaches
the closed form $E=-2\sqrt{t^2+\dV^2}=-2\sqrt{5}\approx-4.472$. The gap between the two curves is
largest here, $\Delta E \approx 2.43$, because $\dV$ acts as an unscreened
one-body potential that the (still weak) correlations barely renormalize.

\paragraph{Strong coupling: the curves converge.}
As $U/t$ grows the two energies draw close together: at $U/t=10$,
$E_{\rm var}/t = -10.101$ ($\dV=0$) vs. $-10.120$ ($\dV=2$), a difference of
only $\approx 0.02$ -- two orders of magnitude smaller than at weak coupling.
In the Mott-like regime the total energy is governed almost entirely by $U$
(both sites approach single occupancy), and the fixed $\dV=2$ site-splitting
becomes a small perturbation on top of the dominant Hubbard physics. The
quasiparticle spectra (3D plots, Fig.~\ref{fig:3d}) show
the same trend: the $\dV=2$ branches, well separated at small $U$, bunch
together with the $\dV=0$-like structure as $U/t$ increases.

\paragraph{Level crossing at $\dV=2$.}
The $\dV=2$ quasiparticle energies show a sharp rearrangement between
$U/t=0.6$ and $U/t=0.7$ (e.g.\ $\Ng=2$: $\epsilon^{qp}\approx-2.00\to-3.12$),
visible as a kink in the 3D plots; this is the level crossing where the
$\dV$-induced one-body gap stops being the dominant scale and the Hubbard
repulsion takes over.

\paragraph{Ghost multiplicity ($\Ng=0$ vs.\ $\Ng=2$).}
The ghost orbitals lower the energy only at larger $U/t$: $E(\Ng{=}0)-E(\Ng{=}2)<10^{-3}$ up to $U/t=2.3$ at $\dV=0$ and $4.3$ at $\dV=2$, and at most $0.13$ in the scanned range (Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}), still well below the error to the exact energy at large $U/t$ ($\approx0.3$--$0.5$).
The ghosts change the transition more than the energy: $U_c/t$ goes from $8$ ($\Ng=0$) to $6.56$ ($\Ng=2$) at $\dV=0$ and the transition is no longer a continuous vanishing of $Z$ (the $\Ng=0$ spectral function disappears with $Z$, the $\Ng=2$ one keeps $Z\ge0.975$ and loses its inner peak, Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}).

\section{The Gutzwiller wave function itself}
\label{sec:psi}

gGA never forms the state it describes. Here it is built explicitly from the converged solutions (script \texttt{gutzwiller\_psi.py}; scans \texttt{psi\_scan.py}, data in \texttt{psi\_scan/}), to read off the ionic and covalent content, the energy and the overlap with the exact ground state (FCI, from \texttt{exact\_dimer\_deltaV.py}).

\paragraph{Construction.} For each fragment, $|\Psi\rangle=P_0P_1|\Psi_{\rm qp}\rangle$, with $|\Psi_{\rm qp}\rangle$ the Slater determinant of the occupied levels of $H_{qp}$ and $P_i=\sum_{\Gamma n}\lambda_{\Gamma n}|\Gamma\rangle\langle n|$ mapping the qp Fock space of the fragment ($4^{N_{\rm eff}}$ states, $N_{\rm eff}=1+\Ng$) onto its four physical configurations $\Gamma$. The matrix is $\lambda=\tilde\Phi\,P^{0\,-1/2}$, with $P^0$ the local density matrix of $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ and $\tilde\Phi$ the embedding state $\Phi$ with the bath particle-hole transformed back, $\tilde\Phi=\Phi\,U_{\rm PH}$, $U_{\rm PH}|n\rangle=(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}f_{j_1}\cdots f_{j_N}|{\rm full}\rangle$.
The phase fixes the relative sign of the covalent and ionic amplitudes (the other choice gives $E_{\rm kin}>0$). Checks: $R=S^{-1}{\rm Tr}[\tilde\Phi^{T}c^\dagger\tilde\Phi f]$ reproduces the $R$ of the solution to $10^{-7}$ (fully for the non-impurity points), and the weight of $\tilde\Phi$ outside the support of $P^0$ is $\le7\times10^{-8}$.
$\Phi$ is the saved $\phi_i$ for non-impurity solutions (\texttt{z.npy}) and the ground state of $H_{\rm imp}$ for impurity solutions. $\Ng=2$, $\dV=2$ uses the non-impurity scan (\texttt{scan\_ng2\_not\_imp\_dV2}): the impurity-solver points (\texttt{scan\_ng2\_dV2\_confirmed}) have the same energies, but at $U/t=0.6,0.7,9.9,10$ the Fermi level is degenerate or the ghosts are inert, $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ does not match $\Phi$, and $\Psi$ fails there.

\paragraph{Normalization and energy.} The gGA constraints give $\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle=1$ only in infinite dimensions; here it grows from $1$ to $\simeq2.8$ with $U/t$ (Tables~\ref{tab:psiE0}, \ref{tab:psiE2}; $0.29$ for $\Ng=2$, $\dV=2$ at small $U/t$, where the ghosts are inert and the value depends on the gauge). All quantities below use the normalized $\Psi$.
The energy $E_\Psi=\langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle$ is variational and far better than the gGA one: for $\Ng=2$ the error is $\le8.5\times10^{-4}$ over the whole scan (against up to $0.4$ for gGA) and the overlap with FCI is $\ge0.9999$. For $\Ng=0$ it is $0.03$ at $U/t=4$ and $0.3$ at $7$ (overlap $0.98$ at $U_c$); $\Ng=0$ at $\dV=0$ is the ordinary Gutzwiller wave function, and gGA's $E$ is its Gutzwiller-approximation value.
Above $U_c$ of $\Ng=0$ ($Z=0$, $R=0$) the solution does not determine $\Psi_{\rm qp}$: the values marked $^\dagger$ (dashed in Fig.~\ref{fig:psi}) depend on the arbitrary $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ stored by the solver (e.g.\ $E_\Psi=-U$ and $P_{\rm ion}=0$ exactly) and are not a result.

\subsection{Role of $R$ in the ionic and covalent character of $\Psi$}
\label{sec:Rmod}
$\Psi$ depends on $R$ through two objects: the qp determinant $\Psi_{\rm qp}$, which is the filled lower half of $H_{qp}$ with hopping $-tR_0R_1$, and the embedding states $\Phi_i$, whose hybridization $V_i$ and bath coupling $\lambda^c_i$ are built from $R$, $\rho$ and $\lambda$ at fixed $\lambda$. To see which of the two sets the ionic weight, $R_i\to s_iR_i$ is changed by hand at a converged solution (script \texttt{psi\_R\_modify.py}, $s_0=s_1=s$, data in \texttt{psi\_scan/Rmod\_*.txt}) in two ways: (A) $R$ only in $H_{qp}$, with the $\Phi_i$ of the solution (so $\lambda=\tilde\Phi P^{0\,-1/2}$ is rebuilt with the new $P^0$), and (B) $R$ everywhere, with $V_i$, $\lambda^c_i$ recomputed and $\Phi_i$ re-solved from $H_{\rm imp}$ (one iteration of the loop with the modified $R$). At $s=1$ both give the $\Psi$ of the previous sections.

\paragraph{$\Ng=0$.} $R_i$ is one number, $R=\sqrt Z$ ($0.866$ at $U/t=4$; $\lambda=0$ at $\dV=0$, which is not saved and was set to zero). Since $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ is then the bonding orbital for any $R$, (A) changes nothing and the whole dependence is in (B). Fig.~\ref{fig:Rmod}a shows that $P_{\rm ion}$ rises smoothly with $R$: $P_{\rm ion}=0.0001$ at $R/R_{\rm gGA}=0.1$, $0.019$ at $0.5$, $0.100$ at $1$ (the value of Sec.~\ref{sec:psi}) and $0.152$ at $1.3$ (exact: $0.146$). Reducing $R$ makes $\Psi$ covalent, $R\to0$ being the Brinkman-Rice limit with no double occupancy, and increasing it makes $\Psi$ ionic. The energy $E_\Psi$ is not stationary at the gGA value of $R$: it is lowest at $R/R_{\rm gGA}\simeq1.3$ ($E_\Psi-E_{\rm FCI}=3\times10^{-4}$ against $0.03$ at $1$, overlap $0.99997$ against $0.9975$) where $P_{\rm ion}=0.15$ is also the exact value. gGA's $R$ minimizes the gGA functional, not $\langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle$, and for $\Ng=0$ it is $\simeq25\%$ too small for $\Psi$; this is the reason why $\Psi_{\Ng=0}$ is too covalent.

\paragraph{$\Ng=2$.} $R_i$ has three components and $|R_i|$ stays within $1.3\%$ of $1$ over the whole scan ($|R|^2=1.000$ at $U/t\le2$, $0.987$ at $8$ for $\dV=0$): the quasiparticle weight no longer decreases, because the ghosts take the correlation, and $R$ only turns in the qp space. With $R_i\to sR_i$, which takes $|R_i|$ away from $1$, (B) leaves $P_{\rm ion}$ unchanged ($0.147$ to $0.143$ for $s=0.7$ to $1.3$ at $U/t=4$, $\dV=0$) whereas (A) changes it from $0.285$ to $0.065$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:Rmod}b): re-solving the embedding compensates the change of $R$, and what (A) shows is only the response of $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ to a $\Phi_i$ that is no longer consistent with it.
The Ng=0 variable has an analogue if $R_i$ is split into the part on the HOMO/LUMO-like orbitals and the rest, which lives on the ghosts. With $\varepsilon_{\rm H}$, $\varepsilon_{\rm L}$ the highest occupied and lowest empty level of $H_{qp}$ and $w^{(i)}_{\rm H}$, $w^{(i)}_{\rm L}$ the parts of their eigenvectors on site $i$, let $\Pi_i$ be the projector on the span of $w^{(i)}_{\rm H}$, $w^{(i)}_{\rm L}$. Then $R_i=\Pi_iR_i+(1-\Pi_i)R_i$ and the change that keeps $|R_i|$ is
\begin{equation}
R_i\;\to\;s\,\Pi_iR_i+\sqrt{|R_i|^2-s^2|\Pi_iR_i|^2}\;\frac{(1-\Pi_i)R_i}{|(1-\Pi_i)R_i|},
\end{equation}
which moves weight between HOMO/LUMO and the ghosts. The weight $|\Pi_iR_i|^2$ on the HOMO/LUMO pair is $0.87$, $0.55$, $0.14$ at $U/t=2,4,6$ for $\dV=0$ and $0.99$, $0.87$, $0.60$ for $\dV=2$: with $U/t$ the weight of $R$ goes to the ghosts. With (A), $P_{\rm ion}$ then follows the Ng=0 rule (Fig.~\ref{fig:Rmod}c): at $\dV=2$ it decreases monotonically when weight is taken from HOMO/LUMO ($0.769\to0.350$ at $U/t=2$, $0.478\to0.227$ at $4$, $0.209\to0.180$ at $6$), at $\dV=0$ it does so below the solution ($0.145\to0.108$ at $U/t=4$ for $s=1\to0.7$). Above the solution, at $\dV=0$, the curves are not monotonic ($U/t=4$: $0.145$, $0.226$, $0.124$ at $s=1,1.15,1.3$), which was not investigated. Taking the weight from the ghosts instead (not norm conserving) makes $P_{\rm ion}$ rise at $\dV=0$ ($0.253\to0.083$ for $s=0.7\to1.3$ at $U/t=4$), the opposite of the behavior of the HOMO/LUMO part. With (B) the change is again at most $\sim0.005$ in the usable range.

In short, for $\Ng=0$ the ionic or covalent character of $\Psi$ is set by $R$ alone, through the embedding state, and a smaller $R$ is more covalent. For $\Ng=2$ the same rule holds for the share of $R$ on the HOMO/LUMO orbitals, but only through $\Psi_{\rm qp}$ and not once the embedding is made consistent with the new $R$: the ghost degrees of freedom of $\Phi_i$ absorb the change.
