\noindent\textbf{Scope.} This note collects the $U/t$ scans produced by the
direct-minimization (``not\_imp'') gGA solver for the half-filled Hubbard dimer,
at zero and finite site-splitting potential $\dV$ (see \texttt{not\_impurity.tex}
for the solver's equations and \texttt{code.tex} for the $\dV$ generalization).
Four scans are collected:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{scan\_ng2\_not\_imp} --- $\Ng=2$, $\dV=0$;
  \item \texttt{scan\_ng4\_not\_imp} --- $\Ng=4$, $\dV=0$;
  \item \texttt{scan\_ng2\_dV2\_confirmed} --- $\Ng=2$, $\dV=2$, cross-checked
        against the impurity-problem solver;
  \item \texttt{scan\_ng4\_dV2\_confirmed} --- $\Ng=4$, $\dV=2$, cross-checked
        against the impurity-problem solver.
\end{itemize}
All scans sweep $U/t \in [0.1,\,10.0]$ in steps of $0.1$, at $t=1$. The
``confirmed'' scans report the lowest-energy solution after reconciling the two
independently implemented solvers (impurity-problem and direct-minimization);
see commit \texttt{fe06bd6}.

\section{Total energy and error vs.\ exact: \texorpdfstring{$\Ng=2$ and $\Ng=4$}{Ng=2 and Ng=4}}
\label{sec:energy}

Figures~\ref{fig:E_dV0} and~\ref{fig:E_dV2} show the variational energy
$E_{\rm var}/t$ of the $\Ng=2$ and $\Ng=4$ solutions together with the exact
(ED) energy, and the error $E_{\rm var}-E_{\rm ED}$ on a logarithmic scale, for
$\dV=0$ and $\dV=2$ respectively. For $\dV=2$ the plotted gGA energies are the
cross-checked (``confirmed'') solutions. The two $\Ng$ curves are
indistinguishable on this scale. At $\dV=0$, $\max|E(\Ng{=}4)-E(\Ng{=}2)|=3\times10^{-8}$.
At $\dV=2$ the two agree to better than $10^{-6}$ for $U/t\lesssim6.2$, while for
$U/t\gtrsim6.3$ the $\Ng=4$ energy is lower by up to $1.3\times10^{-4}$ (at
$U/t=8.4$); this is still negligible against the error to the exact energy, $\approx0.3$--$0.4$ there
(see Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Analysis.}
The number of quasiparticle branches is $2(1+\Ng)$: six for $\Ng=2$ and ten for
$\Ng=4$ (not a doubling). In all four panels the gGA peaks coincide with the
ED ones at small $U/t$ and move away from them as $U/t$ grows, consistent with
the error in the total energy growing with $U/t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}). The $\Ng=4$ ridgelines are visually indistinguishable from the $\Ng=2$ ones
at the same $\dV$: the extra branches carry no visible spectral weight, in line
with the energies, so the gGA peaks do not follow the ED peaks more closely
over the scanned range; the additional branches are the ones discussed as
``sleeping'' ghosts in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}. The effect of $\dV$ is
visible in the same way for both $\Ng$: at $\dV=2$ the branches are well separated
at small $U/t$ and bunch together as $U/t$ increases, with a rearrangement
between $U/t=0.6$ and $0.7$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Quasiparticle orbitals and density: the gap closing at $U_c$}

Two more movies per case show the eigenorbitals of $H_{qp}$ and the quasiparticle density as $U/t$ grows ($\Ng=2,4$; $\dV=0,2$; script \texttt{make\_qp\_orbital\_videos.py}).
They are drawn on the skeleton of the dimer: the two large circles joined left to right are the two states (the direction of $R_s$, the only qp orbital that hops to the other site), and the small outer
circles are their ghosts (the on-site eigenvectors orthogonal to it). Occupation as in the code (\texttt{gga\_dimer.qp\_state}): the lowest $N/2$ eigenvectors returned by \texttt{eigh} are filled,
without fractional occupation. In the orbital movies the occupied orbitals are in the first row and the empty ones in the second, each row in order of increasing energy; circle area is $|\text{amplitude}|^2$ and
the color is the sign (signs are aligned with the previous $U/t$ by maximum overlap). In the density movies the colors give the qp density per orbital (the code's $\rho$), next to the gaps, bond order, $Z$ and double occupancy (or state density
at $\dV=2$) as functions of $U/t$; the dashed line is $U_c/t$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $\Ng=2$, $\dV=0$: \href{run:../scan_ng2_not_imp/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng2_not_imp/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=2$, $\dV=2$: \href{run:../scan_ng2_dV2_confirmed/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng2_dV2_confirmed/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=4$, $\dV=0$: \href{run:../scan_ng4_not_imp/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng4_not_imp/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
  \item $\Ng=4$, $\dV=2$: \href{run:../scan_ng4_dV2_confirmed/qp_orbitals_vs_U.mp4}{orbitals}, \href{run:../scan_ng4_dV2_confirmed/qp_density_vs_U.mp4}{density}
\end{itemize}
What can be seen at the critical $U_c/t$ of the weight transfer ($6.56$ for $\dV=0$, $\simeq8.3$ for $\dV=2$):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Spectral gap} (first panel of the density movies, lowest positive minus highest negative energy among the levels with weight $\ge10^{-3}$): it decreases smoothly with $U/t$
  ($2.0$ at $U/t=0.1$, $1.14$ at $4$, $0.27$ at $6$ for $\dV=0$; $4.39$, $2.0$ (at $U/t=4$) and $1.19$ (at $6$) for $\dV=2$, $\Ng=2$) and closes at $U_c$; the next grid point has a large gap
  ($0.03\to4.94$ between $U/t=6.5$ and $6.6$ at $\dV=0$; $0.001\to4.36$ between $8.5$ and $8.6$ at $\dV=2$, $\Ng=2$; $0.014\to3.30$ between $8.3$ and $8.4$ at $\Ng=4$), and it grows afterwards (at $U/t=10$: $8.59$ and $5.76$).
  The qp gap (HOMO--LUMO) is zero from $U_c$ on; before $U_c$ it follows the spectral gap, except at small $U/t$, where weightless ghost levels lie inside the gap.
  \item \emph{Orbitals}: below $U_c$ the HOMO and LUMO have amplitude on the two states and carry $A(\omega)$ weight; from $U_c$ on, the orbitals at $\epsilon=0$ have no amplitude on the states and zero weight, and sit on ghost circles only
  (two of them at $\Ng=2$, six at $\Ng=4$). Which of these degenerate orbitals are occupied is the choice of \texttt{eigh}, so the occupied ghost orbitals change from frame to frame above $U_c$.
  \item \emph{Bond order and double occupancy} (center--center density matrix element, per spin): both change slope at $U_c$ (at $\dV=0$: bond order $0.17$, $0.09$, $0.08$ and double occupancy $0.040$, $0.016$, $0.013$ at $U/t=6$, $6.5$, $6.6$).
  At $\dV=2$ the bond order has a maximum ($0.40$) at $U/t\simeq4.3$ and falls to $0.08$ at $U_c$.
  \item \emph{State density at $\dV=2$}: the densities of the two states are polarized at small $U/t$ ($0.945$ and $0.055$ at $U/t=0.1$, $0.63$ and $0.37$ at $6$) and become equal at $U_c$ ($0.50$ and $0.50$, $\Ng=2$), staying equal above.
  \item $Z$ stays $\ge0.975$ for all $U/t$ (no collapse of the renormalization), and at $\dV=0$ the state densities are $0.50$ at all $U/t$; the colors of the ghost circles are not a signature of the transition.
\end{itemize}
This is a gap closing and re-opening of the gGA solution. It is not present in the exact dimer, whose spectral gap varies smoothly with $U/t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}).

\subsection{Ionic and covalent weights}

As $U/t$ grows (the analogue of stretching H$_2$) the weight of the ionic configurations (one site doubly occupied, H$^-$H$^+$) is expected to go to the covalent ones (one electron on each site).
The ionic weight is $P_{\rm ion}=\langle n_{0\uparrow}n_{0\downarrow}\rangle+\langle n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow}\rangle$ and the covalent weight is $1-P_{\rm ion}$. In gGA the double occupancies and the site
occupations come from the impurity ground state of each fragment (\texttt{gga\_dimer.impurity\_solve}) at the converged $(R,\lambda)$, as in one iteration of the loop (at $\dV=0$ they reproduce the \texttt{docc} of the solver output);
ED uses the exact ground state ($\Delta V_{\rm ED}=2\,\Delta V_{\rm gGA}$). Figure~\ref{fig:ionic} gives $P_{\rm ion}$ over the whole $U/t$ grid for both $\dV$ and both $\Ng$; the $\Ng=2$ and $\Ng=4$ curves coincide (largest difference $2\times10^{-6}$ at $\dV=0$ and $1.4\times10^{-3}$ at $\dV=2$).

At $\dV=0$ there is no charge transfer ($n=1$ on each site) and the ionic weight falls from $0.5$, i.e.\ the weight moves from ionic to covalent, in ED smoothly ($0.036$ at $U/t=10$). gGA does the same but is too ionic
at intermediate $U/t$ and then drops across $U_c/t\simeq6.56$ ($0.079\to0.022$ between $U/t=6$ and $7$) to an almost purely covalent state ($0.010$ at $U/t=10$). At $\dV=2$ the state is polarized
(H$^-$H$^+$-like, $1.89$ electrons on the favored site) and becomes covalent as $U/t$ grows; gGA is more ionic than ED up to $U/t\simeq7$ and less ionic after $U_c/t\simeq8.3$ ($n=1.021$ against $1.061$ at $U/t=8.5$).
The density of the two states in the density movies matches the physical occupation per spin at the two points checked ($0.945$ at $U/t=0.1$ and $0.630$ at $6$ for $\dV=2$).

\paragraph{Link with the spectral weights.} The hole peaks of $A(\omega)$ are photoemission lines: an electron is removed from the dimer and the cation is left in its bonding (lowest) or
antibonding one-electron orbital; the inner pole is the bonding final state and the outer pole the antibonding one. The intensity of each line is the overlap between that final state and the state obtained by removing an
electron from the ground state. At $\dV=0$ write the ground state as $\cos\theta\,|{\rm cov}\rangle+\sin\theta\,|{\rm ion}\rangle$ ($P_{\rm ion}=\sin^2\theta$). Removing an electron from the covalent part leaves the other electron on the
\emph{other} site, with amplitude $\propto\cos\theta$; removing it from the ionic part leaves the remaining electron on the \emph{same} site, with amplitude $\propto\sin\theta$. The two contributions interfere:
they add in the bonding orbital and subtract in the antibonding one, so that
\[
w_{\rm inner}=(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)^2=1+2\sqrt{P_{\rm ion}(1-P_{\rm ion})},\qquad
w_{\rm outer}=(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)^2=1-2\sqrt{P_{\rm ion}(1-P_{\rm ion})},
\]
which are the exact weights $1\pm4t/\Omega$, $\Omega=\sqrt{U^2+16t^2}$ (checked numerically). For example, at $U/t=4$, $\cos\theta=0.924$ and $\sin\theta=0.383$ ($P_{\rm ion}=0.146$) give $1.707$ and $0.293$.
The imbalance between the two lines is thus the product of the covalent and ionic amplitudes: it is maximal at $U=0$ ($P_{\rm ion}=\tfrac12$, weights $2$ and $0$, the one-electron picture) and it disappears when the state is purely covalent
(both weights equal to $1$, the two Hubbard-band-like lines). Transferring weight from the inner to the outer pole as $U/t$ grows is the same process as transferring weight from ionic to covalent. At $\dV=2$ the same interference holds but the ionic amplitudes of the two sites differ, so the weights depend on three amplitudes
(covalent, ionic on each site).

gGA does not compute the weights this way: they come from $R$ and the quasiparticle spectrum, not from the ionic and covalent amplitudes of the ground state, so the link is only partly kept. Inserting gGA's own composition in the exact
relation gives the inner weight that the gGA state would have; it is compared below with the weight of the lowest-energy peak of the gGA spectrum (nearest to $\epsilon=0$; $\Ng=2$):

\section{Discussion: \texorpdfstring{$\dV=0$}{dV=0} vs. \texorpdfstring{$\dV=2$}{dV=2}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Weak coupling: $\dV$ dominates.}
At small $U/t$ the two scans differ substantially. At $U/t=0.1$,
$E_{\rm var}/t = -2.0503$ for $\dV=0$ but $-4.4823$ for $\dV=2$ (both $\Ng$).
This is the expected $U\to0$ limit: with $\dV=0$ the dimer's noninteracting
energy is $-2t=-2$ plus a small $O(U)$ correction, while at $\dV=2$ the
site-splitting opens a one-body level gap and the $U\to0$ energy approaches
the closed form $E=-2\sqrt{t^2+\dV^2}=-2\sqrt{5}\approx-4.472$. The gap between the two curves is
largest here, $\Delta E \approx 2.43$, because $\dV$ acts as an unscreened
one-body potential that the (still weak) correlations barely renormalize.

\paragraph{Strong coupling: the curves converge.}
As $U/t$ grows the two energies draw close together: at $U/t=10$,
$E_{\rm var}/t = -10.101$ ($\dV=0$) vs. $-10.120$ ($\dV=2$), a difference of
only $\approx 0.02$ -- two orders of magnitude smaller than at weak coupling.
In the Mott-like regime the total energy is governed almost entirely by $U$
(both sites approach single occupancy), and the fixed $\dV=2$ site-splitting
becomes a small perturbation on top of the dominant Hubbard physics. The
quasiparticle spectra (3D plots, Fig.~\ref{fig:3d}) show
the same trend: the $\dV=2$ branches, well separated at small $U$, bunch
together with the $\dV=0$-like structure as $U/t$ increases.

\paragraph{Level crossing at $\dV=2$.}
The $\dV=2$ quasiparticle energies show a sharp rearrangement between
$U/t=0.6$ and $U/t=0.7$ (e.g.\ $\Ng=2$: $\epsilon^{qp}\approx-2.00\to-3.12$),
visible as a kink in the 3D plots; this is the level crossing where the
$\dV$-induced one-body gap stops being the dominant scale and the Hubbard
repulsion takes over.

\paragraph{Ghost multiplicity ($\Ng=2$ vs. $\Ng=4$).}
At both $\dV=0$ and $\dV=2$, doubling the ghost multiplicity from $\Ng=2$ to
$\Ng=4$ leaves the lowest variational energy numerically unchanged at $\dV=0$ over the
whole scanned range, and at $\dV=2$ for $U/t\lesssim6.2$ (for larger $U/t$ the $\Ng=4$ energy
is lower by at most $1.3\times10^{-4}$, Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}): at $\dV=0$,
$E(\Ng{=}4)-E(\Ng{=}2)=0$ to the printed precision at essentially every $U/t$
(largest deviation $\sim3\times10^{-8}$, at the level of solver tolerance), and
the confirmed energies show the same agreement at $\dV=2$ (e.g.\ $U/t=1.0$:
$-4.593786$ vs.\ $-4.593786$). The extra ghost
degrees of freedom introduced at $\Ng=4$ therefore stay essentially energetically inert
for this dimer at both values of $\dV$ in this $U$ range -- consistent with
the known $\Ng\ge2$ ``sleeping ghost'' caveat:
the additional branches exist in the quasiparticle spectrum (compare
Fig.~\ref{fig:3d}, which shows ten instead of six
$\epsilon^{qp}_n$ branches at $\Ng=4$) but do not lower the ground-state
energy below what $\Ng=2$ already finds.
